River Sauce — a navigable digital archive + final pitch deck

A “What If?” brief asked us to design for the year 2125. We answered with River Sauce — a supplement brand that claims to rebuild your liver, presented as if it had been part of everyday life for a century.

The work is deliberately plausible rather than flashy: real research borrowed just enough to make a fictional product believable, then staged through the brand’s own website — a digital archive a visitor in 2125 might actually browse. My focus was the final realisation: compositing the hero image, building the website archive, and refining the pitch deck so the whole story held together.

2025 was for healthier guts, 2075 healthier livers.

01

Frame the “what if?”

We started from real signals — the history of the hamburger as a “new food”, what people reach for at the edge of life, and a 100-mile diet forced by environmental collapse. Run through the Futures Cone, these settled our concept as a plausible 2125 where human biology is upgraded to meet old desires.

02

Pivot toward plausibility

An early direction built the brand on a “hidden truth” — a flipped interface that exposed a darker reality. Tutor feedback challenged the logic, so we reframed the website as a genuine digital archive instead of a gimmick. The audience sharpened too: older drinkers who still click webpage links, care about their liver, and want one simple spoonful that works.

03

Borrow real science

To make the fiction believable without making a literal medical claim, we borrowed three real mechanisms and retold them in brand language: plant-cell bioencapsulation as Protect, edible-plant nanoparticles as Deliver, and broccoli-sprout detox markers as Detox. Each became a “science behind” page in the archive.

04

Build the hero image

From Fiona’s bottle model I directed and composited the hero: keeping the tapered, slightly uncanny profile and high-gloss finish, then adding a floating liquid-metal background and shifting the bottle from upright to lying. Modelled in Blender and Cinema 4D, finished in Photoshop — the single image that sets the brand’s tone across the site and deck.

05

Resolve as an archive

The website became the outcome — worldbuilding, product and “science behind” pages folded into one experience a 2125 visitor could navigate. I then built the matching pitch deck from the same system, so the story read consistently from landing page to final slide.

A believable future is mostly editing — keeping the few real things that make a fiction hold.
